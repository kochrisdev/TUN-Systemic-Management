# TUN Systemic Management — Manifesto v0.1

[Documentation index](README.md) · [Specification](SPECIFICATION-v0.1.md)

Intelligence can increasingly participate in planning, analysis, coordination, and action. Organizations remain responsible for the purposes they pursue and the consequences they create. We propose twelve commitments for managing that relationship.

## 01 — Begin with outcomes

Describe the change that matters to people. Treat activities and outputs as possible contributors to that change. Check whether the change actually occurred.

## 02 — Make purpose and priorities explicit

Explain why an initiative deserves attention and resources. Record tradeoffs, constraints, and the work that will wait. Revisit priorities when their assumptions change.

## 03 — Separate recommendation, decision, authority, and execution

An AI recommendation is input to a decision. A decision requires an accountable owner. Delegated authority has limits. Each consequential action still needs current authorization at the point where it occurs.

## 04 — Delegate objectives with boundaries

Give people and agents a clear mandate together with scope, resources, expiry, escalation conditions, and ways to intervene. Any further delegation stays within the original authority and shared limits.

## 05 — Keep human accountability identifiable

Name the person responsible for a mandate, decision, exception, or unresolved problem. AI participation does not remove the responsibilities of the people and organizations deploying it.

## 06 — Manage AI as a visible participant

Make an agent's purpose, capabilities, authority, dependencies, and relevant history inspectable. Judge its contribution in the context of the people and systems around it.

## 07 — Require evidence proportional to consequence

State what a claim means and what supports it. Preserve evidence scope, freshness, source, and limitations. The strength of a management claim should reflect the strength of its evidence.

## 08 — Preserve disagreement and uncertainty

Make room for contrary observations and affected people's concerns. Keep unknown outcomes visible. Fluency, consensus, and model confidence do not settle a contested fact.

## 09 — Manage a portfolio of revisable commitments

Allocate resources in increments that can be reviewed. Compare alternatives, opportunity costs, and dependencies. Continue an initiative because its current evidence warrants it.

## 10 — Measure value, risk, cost, and learning together

Account for quality, human effort, operational reliability, harmful effects, and recovery work alongside speed. Use metrics to support decisions and examine how incentives can distort them.

## 11 — Design escalation and intervention with delegation

Identify who can respond, what can be stopped, and what may already have happened. Treat acknowledgement of a stop request and evidence that it worked as separate facts.

## 12 — Adapt and retire deliberately

Turn reviewed evidence into changes in decisions, policies, and operating practice. Preserve the reasoning behind those changes. End mandates and systems when their purpose or authority expires, and account for outstanding effects.

**Purpose. Bounded delegation. Evidence-led adaptation.**
