# Human–AI accountability v0.1

Status: draft guidance. [Documentation index](README.md)

## Named responsibilities

| Responsibility | Accountable human role | AI assistance may include |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome ownership | Product or service owner | Analyzing signals and proposing measures |
| Investment decision | Authorized sponsor or budget owner | Comparing options and resource scenarios |
| Mandate and delegation | Authorized delegator | Drafting boundaries and identifying ambiguities |
| Operational enforcement | Application or service owner | Preparing candidate actions and checking records |
| Evidence review | Named reviewer with relevant competence | Collecting permitted evidence and summarizing limitations |
| Incident response | Designated responder | Detecting conditions, assembling context, recommending containment |

One person may hold several roles in a small team. Record those overlaps. Independent review is useful when consequences, conflicts of interest, or domain practice warrant it; recording a second AI opinion is not the same as assigning an independent accountable human.

## Agent participation

An agent needs an identifiable purpose, owner, capabilities, data access, delegated authority, review conditions, and operating history. Its tool access and credentials belong behind actual enforcement. Organizational language such as “manager” or “lead” does not grant additional permissions.

The deploying organization retains responsibility for assigning reasonable tasks, providing usable evidence, and acting on failures. Model and provider changes should trigger review when they materially affect the evidence or controls on which a mandate depends.

## Human participation

Include the people affected by the workflow in framing outcomes and reviewing results. Let reviewers report ambiguity, challenge assumptions, and escalate without having to manufacture certainty. Preserve dissent and explain how a decision addressed it.

Measure the effort required to supervise the system. Frequent approvals can become ceremonial if reviewers lack time, context, or authority to refuse. Review capacity is a resource constraint and should shape the scope of delegation.

Team-level improvement metrics should not silently become individual employee rankings. Any different use of collected information needs its own purpose, access rules, and organizational decision.
