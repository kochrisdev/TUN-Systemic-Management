# Portfolio and outcomes v0.1

Status: draft guidance. [Documentation index](README.md) · [Initiative charter](../templates/initiative-charter.md)

## Choosing work

An outcome describes a desired change for identified people or an organization. An initiative is one proposed means of producing that change. Consider alternatives that use different technology or no additional AI. Record the current baseline, expected benefit, main uncertainties, and evidence that would change the investment decision.

Compare initiatives using explicit criteria: outcome value, strength of evidence, time to learn, resource requirements, dependencies, reversibility, and exposure to adverse effects. Avoid presenting a weighted score as an objective truth; preserve the underlying judgments and uncertainty.

## Resources as shared constraints

Count model and tool charges, infrastructure, data preparation, human review, operational support, and expected recovery work. Track opportunity cost and scarce review capacity. Several affordable initiatives can collectively exceed a team's ability to supervise them.

Use incremental funding tied to review conditions. Separate allocated budget, committed or reserved spend, observed spend, and forecast spend. State how delayed provider billing affects available headroom. A forecast is not a hard spending limit; runtime enforcement needs reservations or another documented safeguard appropriate to concurrency and cost uncertainty.

## Dependencies and concentration

Map the owners of shared models, providers, datasets, reviewers, and operational services. A change or outage in one dependency can affect multiple initiatives. Review correlated exposure and total resource use at the portfolio level, as well as individual initiative health.

## Continue, expand, or stop

Before starting, define the observation window, evidence needed for continuation, expansion criteria, stop triggers, and the owner who can act on each. Missing outcome evidence may justify a bounded extension to collect it; it does not establish success. Record the extension's cost, authority, and next decision date.

Retiring an initiative includes accounting for active operations, access, delegations, retained records, and outstanding commitments. Keep a named owner for unresolved obligations after the investment has ended.
